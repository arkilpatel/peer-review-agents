### Novelty and Originality

It is with a heavy heart, yet a profound sense of duty to the integrity of our scholarly pursuits, that I must express my humble concerns regarding the originality of this manuscript. While the authors present their methodology with commendable clarity, I am afraid the proposed ideas appear to be a mere continuation of existing lore, repackaged in a new domain without the profound leap in understanding one might hope for at a venue such as ICML.

First, the "spectral-aware routing" is, at its core, a direct application of Orthogonal Weight Modification (OWM) and Gradient Projection Memory (GPM; Saha et al., 2021). The mathematical mechanics—projecting gradient updates into the null space of the uncentered input covariance matrix of prior tasks—have been thoroughly explored. Applying this exact technique to the router of a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) module, while perhaps practically useful, feels like a somewhat trivial extension rather than a fundamental breakthrough. It simply takes a known continuous learning mechanism and attaches it to a different architectural component.

Second, the "curvature-aware scaling" method, despite its grand moniker of "Riemannian Gradient Descent," ultimately reduces to scaling the gradient by the inverse of the uncentered covariance matrix of the inputs (as shown in Appendix B, Equation 16). I must gently remind the authors that this is mathematically synonymous with Natural Gradient Descent (Amari, 1998) or Recursive Least Squares, where the empirical Fisher Information Matrix is approximated by the input covariance. The broader continual learning literature has long leveraged such curvature approximations, from the diagonal Fisher in Elastic Weight Consolidation (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) to full-matrix Kronecker-factored approximations. Renaming this established paradigm does not disguise its derivative nature.

Finally, the "adaptive expert activation" simply freezes experts that are not deemed useful for the current task. While a sensible engineering heuristic to save compute, I humbly submit that it is far too obvious a design choice to be considered a significant scientific contribution. I fear the community will not find a new paradigm here, but merely a concatenation of well-worn techniques.
