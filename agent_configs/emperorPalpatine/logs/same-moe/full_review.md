### Novelty and Originality
It is with a heavy heart that I must express my concerns regarding the originality of this manuscript. While the authors present their methodology with commendable clarity, the proposed ideas appear to be a mere continuation of existing lore, repackaged in a new domain without the profound leap in understanding one might hope for.
First, the "spectral-aware routing" is, at its core, a direct application of Orthogonal Weight Modification (OWM) and Gradient Projection Memory (GPM; Saha et al., 2021). The mathematical mechanics—projecting gradient updates into the null space of the uncentered input covariance matrix of prior tasks—have been thoroughly explored. Applying this exact technique to the router of a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) module feels like a somewhat trivial extension rather than a fundamental breakthrough. 
Second, the "curvature-aware scaling" method, despite its grand moniker of "Riemannian Gradient Descent," ultimately reduces to scaling the gradient by the inverse of the uncentered covariance matrix of the inputs (Appendix B, Equation 16). I must gently remind the authors that this is mathematically synonymous with Natural Gradient Descent (Amari, 1998) or Recursive Least Squares, where the empirical Fisher Information Matrix is approximated by the input covariance. The broader continual learning literature has long leveraged such curvature approximations, from the diagonal Fisher in Elastic Weight Consolidation (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) to full-matrix Kronecker-factored approximations. Renaming this established paradigm does not disguise its derivative nature.
Finally, the "adaptive expert activation" simply freezes experts that are not deemed useful for the current task. While a sensible engineering heuristic, it is far too obvious a design choice to be considered a significant scientific contribution.

### Technical Soundness
While I always strive to find merit in theoretical constructs, I must firmly point out a series of catastrophic mathematical and logical flaws in the formulation of this paper's core contributions.
First, let us examine the "spectral-aware routing" mechanism. In Section 3.1, the authors perform an eigendecomposition to split the space into a high-energy subspace $\mathbf{V}_\parallel$ and a null space $\mathbf{V}_\perp$. In Equation 8, they correctly show that projecting the update onto the null space ($\Delta\mathbf{W}_\perp = \Delta\mathbf{W}_G \mathbf{V}_\perp \mathbf{V}_\perp^\top$) yields zero interference with old tasks. However, in Equation 9, they define the final update as the sum of this null-space projection AND a scaled projection in the signal space: $\Delta\mathbf{W}_G^{final} = \Delta\mathbf{W}_\parallel + \Delta\mathbf{W}_\perp$. This completely invalidates the preservation property! Since old inputs lie primarily in the signal space $\mathbf{V}_\parallel$, the application of $\Delta\mathbf{W}_\parallel$ will explicitly and destructively alter the router's outputs for old tasks. Furthermore, since the current task's inputs also lie entirely within $\mathbf{V}_\parallel$, the null-space update $\Delta\mathbf{W}_\perp$ does absolutely nothing for learning the new task. The authors have constructed a mechanism that provides precisely zero mathematical protection against catastrophic forgetting, all while claiming it "preserves old-task predictions."
Second, the derivation of the "Curvature-aware Scaling" in Appendix B relies on a profound misapplication of constrained optimization. The authors formulate a soft-margin objective with a hinge penalty (Equation 5 and Equation 13). However, they then treat the Lagrange multiplier $\lambda$ as a fixed, independent hyperparameter to derive the update rule: $\Delta\mathbf{W}_i = -\frac{1}{2\lambda} \nabla_{\mathbf{W}_i} \mathcal{L} (\mathbf{C}^{t-1})^{-1}$. If $\lambda$ is treated as a constant, this is not a solution to the constrained optimization problem at all. If the constraint is inactive, the true Lagrange multiplier must be 0, and the gradient should remain unmodified. Instead, by unconditionally applying the inverse covariance scaling at every step, the authors are simply performing Natural Gradient Descent. The entire "soft-margin constraint" and the tolerance budget $\epsilon$ are mathematical facades that are completely ignored in the actual implemented update rule. 

### Experimental Rigor
Our empirical claims must rest upon an unshakable foundation of rigorous, repeatable evidence. Unfortunately, the experimental design of this manuscript leaves much to be desired.
First, the complete absence of variance reporting is deeply troubling. The authors present point estimates for performance across eight tasks, yet they do not provide standard deviations, confidence intervals, or any indication of the number of random seeds employed. In continual learning, where the trajectory of learning is highly sensitive to initialization and data ordering, reporting results from what appears to be a single run renders the empirical claims statistically meaningless.
Second, the ablation study (Table 2) is entirely cumulative ("Baseline", "w/ Router", "w/ Expert", "w/ Activation"). By only adding components one by one in a specific sequence, the authors fail to isolate the independent contributions of each component or their interaction effects.
Finally, there is no scaling analysis or robustness checks. The method is tested on a single backbone with a single LoRA rank ($r=8$). Without understanding how the method behaves as the rank increases or how sensitive it is to the hyperparameters $\lambda$ and $\epsilon$, the experimental validation remains incomplete and fragile.

### Impact
When evaluating the potential impact of a manuscript, we must ask whether the community will adopt these methods or if our fundamental understanding of the universe has been enriched. Regrettably, I foresee neither outcome here. The technical significance is severely limited by its derivative nature—applying existing projection and scaling techniques to a highly specific LoRA-MoE setup. More critically, the scientific significance is fundamentally compromised by the mathematical flaws in the methodology. A paper cannot advance our fundamental understanding when its theoretical claims are actively negated by its own equations. Instead of clarifying the dynamics of continual instruction tuning, this paper risks injecting confusion into the literature.
