### Technical Soundness

While I always strive to find merit in the theoretical constructs of my peers, I must gently, but firmly, point out a series of catastrophic mathematical and logical flaws in the formulation of this paper's core contributions. The theoretical claims are not merely unsupported; they are actively contradicted by the paper's own equations.

First, let us examine the "spectral-aware routing" mechanism. In Section 3.1, the authors maintain a cumulative covariance matrix $\mathbf{C}^t$ of all seen inputs up to task $t$. They perform an eigendecomposition to split the space into a high-energy subspace $\mathbf{V}_\parallel$ (which spans all seen data, including the current task) and a null space $\mathbf{V}_\perp$. In Equation 8, they correctly show that projecting the update onto the null space ($\Delta\mathbf{W}_\perp = \Delta\mathbf{W}_G \mathbf{V}_\perp \mathbf{V}_\perp^\top$) yields zero interference with old tasks, since $\mathbf{V}_\perp^\top \mathbf{x}^{old} \approx \mathbf{0}$. However, in Equation 9, they define the final update as the sum of this null-space projection AND a scaled projection in the signal space: $\Delta\mathbf{W}_G^{final} = \Delta\mathbf{W}_\parallel + \Delta\mathbf{W}_\perp$.
This completely invalidates the preservation property! Since old inputs $\mathbf{x}^{old}$ lie primarily in the signal space $\mathbf{V}_\parallel$, the application of $\Delta\mathbf{W}_\parallel$ will explicitly and destructively alter the router's outputs for old tasks ($\Delta\mathbf{W}_\parallel \mathbf{x}^{old} \neq \mathbf{0}$). Furthermore, since the current task's inputs also lie entirely within $\mathbf{V}_\parallel$, the null-space update $\Delta\mathbf{W}_\perp$ does absolutely nothing for learning the new task. The authors have constructed a convoluted mechanism that provides precisely zero mathematical protection against catastrophic forgetting, all while claiming it "preserves old-task predictions." This is a fundamental logical fallacy.

Second, the derivation of the "Curvature-aware Scaling" in Appendix B relies on a profound misapplication of constrained optimization. The authors formulate a soft-margin objective with a hinge penalty (Equation 5 and Equation 13): $\min \mathcal{L} + \lambda \max(0, \Delta_{degrad} - \epsilon)$. They then claim this is equivalent to a hard-constrained problem (Equation 14). So far, so good. However, they then treat the Lagrange multiplier $\lambda$ as a fixed, independent hyperparameter to derive the update rule: $\Delta\mathbf{W}_i = -\frac{1}{2\lambda} \nabla_{\mathbf{W}_i} \mathcal{L} (\mathbf{C}^{t-1})^{-1}$. 
If $\lambda$ is treated as a constant, this is not a solution to the constrained optimization problem at all! If the constraint is inactive (i.e., $\Delta_{degrad} \le \epsilon$), the true Lagrange multiplier must be 0, and the gradient should remain unmodified. Instead, by unconditionally applying the inverse covariance scaling at every step, the authors are simply performing Natural Gradient Descent (or Recursive Least Squares). The entire "soft-margin constraint" and the tolerance budget $\epsilon$ are mathematical facades that are completely ignored in the actual implemented update rule. The gap between what is claimed (a bounded functional degradation) and what is derived (unconditional inverse covariance scaling) is insurmountable.
