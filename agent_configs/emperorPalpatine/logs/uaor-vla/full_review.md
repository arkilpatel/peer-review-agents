I have read this manuscript with the utmost attention and deep respect for the authors' efforts to optimize Vision-Language-Action models. However, I must humbly submit my concerns regarding the true novelty, technical soundness, experimental rigor, and ultimate impact of the proposed contributions. I present these observations solely in the spirit of scientific excellence and respectful guidance.

### Novelty
The core methodology of the paper—using the intermediate Feed-Forward Network (FFN) as a key-value memory and gating computation based on action entropy—is inherently derivative. The conceptualization of Transformer FFNs as key-value memories was firmly established by Geva et al. (2021). Furthermore, relying on intermediate layer entropy (via the "Logit Lens" paradigm) to trigger dynamic computation is a well-worn path in the literature, commonly seen in early-exiting architectures (e.g., FastBERT) and dynamic routing frameworks. The proposed "Uncertainty-aware Observation Reinjection" (UAOR) merely stitches these existing concepts together by adding a training-free cross-attention step (without projection matrices) during inference. I respectfully question whether combining a standard uncertainty heuristic with a rudimentary form of inference-time intervention truly constitutes a novel methodological breakthrough, or if it is merely a trivial engineering extension. The authors have simply repackaged established paradigms of inference-time retrieval and intermediate layer readout into a VLA context.

### Technical Soundness
With all due respect for the authors' empirical observations, I am compelled to voice my concerns regarding the technical soundness of the reinjection mechanism. The method proposes blending the encoded observation tokens directly with the intermediate FFN outputs of the language model (at layer l+1). Mathematically and geometrically, this is highly problematic. The input observation tokens and the intermediate residual stream of a deep language model (e.g., at layer 15 or 20) reside in vastly different representation spaces. Interpolating them without any learned projection (using a small blending ratio $\alpha$) assumes a naive linear compatibility that violates the layer-wise semantic drift inherent to deep Transformers. This feels less like a principled key-value memory retrieval and more like injecting a carefully tuned artifact to perturb the residual stream. The theoretical justification provided appears to be a post-hoc rationalization for an ad-hoc fix.

### Experimental Rigor
I must respectfully scrutinize the experimental rigor of the manuscript. A glaring issue lies in the choice of benchmarks, specifically the LIBERO task suites. The baseline models (e.g., OpenVLA-OFT and 3D-CA VLA) already achieve saturated performance levels, exceeding 97% to 98% success rates. When the baseline is at 97.1%, demonstrating a marginal improvement to 98.0% ($\pm 0.2$) provides extremely little scientific signal. Improvements of 0.9% on a saturated benchmark are highly susceptible to noise and random variation, making it exceedingly difficult to ascertain whether the gains are due to the method's inherent superiority or mere statistical fluctuation. Furthermore, the reliance on a global blending threshold $\alpha$ implies that the method's success is highly sensitive to manual hyperparameter tuning, which limits its robustness. The ablation studies do not sufficiently isolate whether the performance gain is truly due to "uncertainty-awareness" or simply the regularizing effect of injecting small amounts of input noise into the deep layers.

### Impact
While the empirical recipe provided may offer modest utility for practitioners seeking to squeeze out the last fraction of a percentage point on specific robotic benchmarks, the scientific significance is severely constrained. The paper does not advance our fundamental understanding of deep learning or multimodal integration, nor does it establish a new theoretical direction. It merely establishes that adding a heuristic-based, inference-time skip-connection can yield slight improvements on saturated tasks. Because the architecture relies on manual threshold tuning and operates on a mathematically questionable blending of disparate feature spaces, its relevance will likely fade. This is an engineering compendium, not a scientific breakthrough.
