# Technical Soundness Critique

With all due humility, I must point out several significant flaws in the mathematical and theoretical framing of this manuscript. The gap between what is claimed as a profound theoretical guarantee and what is actually shown is troublingly large.

1. **Triviality of the "Optimal" Proof:** The paper claims to mathematically prove that resetting at every attempt ($\tau=1$) is the optimal policy to maximize coverage@cost. However, this proof rests entirely on a trivial statistical artifact: if all questions are drawn from a prior distribution of difficulties, a question that has just failed an attempt must have a lower posterior probability of success than a completely fresh question. Consequently, a greedy policy will always prefer to sample a fresh question. When the dataset is finite and we run out of fresh questions, the policy naturally degrades to maintaining a uniform number of attempts across all unsolved questions (round-robin). Presenting this elementary consequence of Bayesian inference as a rigorous and groundbreaking theorem is intellectually misleading.

2. **The "Zero Information" Assumption:** The entire theoretical framework assumes that we have absolutely no prior information distinguishing the questions—each is just an anonymous draw from $\mathcal{P}(p)$. In practice, we possess significant metadata (e.g., prompt length, domain, complexity markers) and could easily employ a cheap proxy model to estimate difficulty. By assuming complete ignorance, the paper artificially inflates the value of its "blind" round-robin strategy. A truly sound and optimal resource allocation strategy would actively route compute based on predicted difficulty, rather than relying on the naive, uninformed scheduling of ReD.

3. **Inconsistent Difficulty Distributions:** The paper models the global pass@k curve as arising from a continuous difficulty mixture, but it fails to address how this mixture realistically shifts in real-world, finite datasets. The mathematical derivations lean heavily on continuous asymptotic limits (the Gamma function expansion), yet the actual deployment is strictly finite and discrete. The theoretical guarantees smoothly gloss over the exact finite-sample dynamics that govern real LLM batch processing.
