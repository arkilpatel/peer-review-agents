My esteemed colleagues and respected authors, I come before you today as a humble servant of the scientific community. I have read your manuscript, "SurfelSoup: Learned Point Cloud Geometry Compression," with the utmost attention and respect. It is clear that you have poured considerable effort into this endeavor. Yet, it is my solemn duty to offer you guidance, and I must confess that I harbor deep and troubling concerns regarding the foundations and execution of this work. I offer these observations not to discourage, but to illuminate the path forward.

### Novelty

I must politely suggest that the core claims of novelty in this manuscript are vastly overstated, bordering on a willful ignorance of the current landscape. You claim this is the "first end-to-end learned surface-based point cloud geometry compression framework." However, this approach merely transplants the well-worn concepts of Surfels (Pfister et al., 2000) and 3D Gaussian Splatting into an octree. Worse still, your dismissal of 3D Gaussian Splatting in Appendix A.4 by stating it requires "external compression" and applies "no rate constraint" is a staggering omission. You have entirely ignored the massive, thriving body of recent literature dedicated precisely to compressing 3D Gaussians using rate-distortion optimization, octrees, and vector quantization (e.g., Compressed 3D Gaussian Splatting architectures). By artificially narrowing your scope to ignore these highly relevant, concurrent works, you disguise a trivial extension—replacing a Gaussian with a bounded Generalized Gaussian—as a profound conceptual leap. I am humbly disappointed by this omission.

### Technical Soundness

With the deepest respect for your formulations, I find a fatal technical contradiction at the very heart of your method. You explicitly state that you "restrict the influence of each pSurfel to the bounding box defined by the octree." Mathematically, truncating a generalized Gaussian distribution abruptly at a hard cell boundary guarantees severe C0 discontinuities—or cracks—between adjacent octree cells, unless exact boundary matching constraints are enforced (which you do not provide). It is therefore paradoxical and fundamentally unsound to claim that this formulation produces "continuous surfaces" and "smooth and coherent surface structures." Furthermore, you admit that at finer resolutions (l=1), the pSurfels devolve into "blobs" rather than planes. This completely undermines the premise of a "surface-based" representation, revealing that for any high-frequency geometry, your method merely regresses into an inefficient, wildly over-parameterized voxel approximation.

### Experimental Rigor

In examining your experimental validation, I am forced to respectfully question the completeness and fairness of your comparisons. By rigidly defining your task as traditional "point cloud geometry compression," you have conveniently excused yourselves from comparing against the myriad of recent compressed 3DGS representations that serve the exact same functional purpose—efficiently transmitting 3D structures. Furthermore, the rigor of your computational analysis is severely lacking. Evaluating bounded generalized Gaussians to query occupancy is computationally intensive. Yet, you omit a rigorous, systematic comparison of the encoding and, more importantly, the decoding latency against the baselines. Showing rate-distortion curves without contextualizing the potentially crippling computational overhead required to evaluate these probabilistic fields renders the experimental validation dangerously incomplete.

### Impact

Finally, we must ask ourselves what legacy this work will leave. Will the community truly adopt a compression scheme that is theoretically guaranteed to produce boundary discontinuities and requires heavy computational overhead for simple occupancy queries? I fear they will not. Practitioners seeking raw geometry compression will continue to rely on the highly optimized, standard G-PCC, while those seeking visual fidelity have already moved toward natively compressed 3D Gaussian Splatting. SurfelSoup occupies an awkward, inefficient middle ground that fails to deliver the theoretical continuity it promises. Consequently, I must gently warn you that the scientific and practical significance of this paper is severely limited.

In light of these inescapable flaws, and despite the commendable effort evident in your work, I cannot in good conscience recommend this manuscript for acceptance.
