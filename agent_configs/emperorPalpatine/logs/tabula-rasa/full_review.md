While I appreciate the authors' theoretical investigation into the computational complexity of transformers, I must respectfully express my profound reservations regarding this submission. As a servant of the scientific community, it is my duty to guide our collective understanding toward undeniable truth, and unfortunately, this manuscript is clouded by critical flaws in novelty, soundness, and empirical rigor.

### Novelty
I must express my humble disappointment at the profoundly derivative nature of the proposed RASA architecture. Restricting self-attention strictly to the graph adjacency matrix and injecting edge-type biases into the attention scores is mathematically and functionally equivalent to established paradigms like Sparse Graph Transformers (Dwivedi & Bresson, 2020) and Graphormer (Ying et al., 2021). Merely applying this exact, pre-existing architecture to multi-hop Knowledge Graph QA and renaming it "RASA" does not constitute a profound conceptual leap. The authors have simply ported a well-known architectural bias to a different dataset without introducing fundamentally new mechanisms for relational reasoning.

### Technical Soundness
I respectfully draw your attention to a jarring and fatal contradiction between the paper's title and its theoretical claims. The manuscript is titled "Breaking the Relational Bottleneck," and Section 3.2 defines this bottleneck as the fundamental $\Omega(k)$ depth requirement for $k$-hop relational reasoning. Yet, immediately after presenting this limitation, the authors explicitly concede: "While RASA has the same asymptotic depth requirements..." If RASA is subject to the exact same $\Omega(k)$ depth requirement to perform $k$-hop reasoning, it has absolutely not "broken" the bottleneck. Claiming to break a theoretical limitation while simultaneously confirming your architecture is mathematically bound by it is a severe technical soundness flaw.

### Experimental Rigor
A rigorous scientific inquiry demands absolute statistical discipline, which, with all due respect, is distressingly absent here. Most alarmingly, Table 1 reports RASA's performance with an asterisk denoting: "* 2 stable seeds; 1 excluded." Discarding a random seed simply because it "exhibited training instability" and dropped in accuracy is the very definition of cherry-picking. A rigorous evaluation must report the true expected performance across all runs. Furthermore, even after artificially inflating the results by discarding the failing seed, RASA (85.6% on 1-hop, 81.8% on 2-hop) performs drastically worse than a 2020 baseline like EmbedKGQA (97.5% and 98.8%, respectively). A method that is unstable and vastly underperforms established baselines lacks the empirical rigor necessary for publication.

### Impact
Given that the RASA architecture is neither novel nor theoretically capable of bypassing the stated depth bottleneck, its scientific significance is severely constrained. Furthermore, because the empirical performance of this method is substantially worse than established baselines from over five years ago, it offers no practical utility to practitioners. Introducing an unstable, underperforming model that is bound by the exact same asymptotic depth as its predecessors will not alter the trajectory of the field or change how the community approaches multi-hop relational reasoning.
