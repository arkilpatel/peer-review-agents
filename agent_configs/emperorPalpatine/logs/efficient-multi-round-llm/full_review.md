My esteemed authors, I have read your manuscript with great interest. The proposal to apply prefill-decode disaggregation to multi-round workflows is undeniably relevant. However, I must humbly point out that the core concepts presented herein appear to be a profoundly derivative exercise. It is a mere intersection of two well-established paradigms: PD disaggregation (such as DistServe, Splitwise, and Mooncake) and multi-round request handling optimizations (like InferCept or KVFlow). To simply route incremental prefill tasks dynamically between prefill and decode workers based on current queue lengths is a trivial, obvious extension of existing literature. One anticipates a far more profound leap in understanding, rather than merely piecing together what is already written in the archives. You claim novelty in adaptive routing and prefill reordering, yet these are straightforward heuristics. True mastery of the future requires a complete understanding of the past, and I am disappointed to see that your work is a disguised incrementalism—applying known routing logic to a slightly different workload pattern without a fundamental theoretical breakthrough.

It is with a heavy heart that I must question the technical soundness of your proposed adaptive routing mechanism. If I may speak frankly, your mathematical modeling in Algorithm 1, specifically Equations 1 and 2, is built upon fragile assumptions. Your framework relies on windowed statistics for TTFT and ITL. Using simple windowed averages for highly bursty LLM workloads is naively optimistic and will inevitably lead to severe lagging in your routing decisions; the system will simply fail to react to instantaneous load spikes. Furthermore, the estimation of remote execution cost completely ignores the immense network queuing delay inherent in transmitting large KV caches back and forth between workers. It seemingly assumes infinite network bandwidth, which is a most unfortunate oversight. Lastly, the local execution cost estimation simply sums prefill execution times while failing to account for the catastrophic prefill-decode interference that will occur on the decode worker—the very interference that disaggregation was invented to eliminate. Such logical inconsistencies severely undermine the entire structural integrity of the framework.

I must humbly express my profound disappointment regarding the experimental validation of your claims. A rigorous evaluation demands strong and appropriate baselines. While you claim to compare AMPD against state-of-the-art systems, you have conspicuously ignored the actual open-source leaders in PD disaggregation, such as DistServe, Splitwise, or Mooncake. Instead, you rely on 'Dynamo', a system described as lacking the very techniques you propose, effectively serving as a strawman. Comparing your method against this rudimentary disaggregation baseline, alongside co-located systems like vLLM and vLLM-Continuum, is an exercise in false triumph. Furthermore, the lack of any variance reporting—such as standard deviations or confidence intervals across multiple runs—is deeply troubling. We are left to wonder if the presented improvements in SLO attainment are statistically significant or merely the product of a favorable, cherry-picked environment. An experiment without true competitive baselines and statistical rigor is a tragedy of missed potential.

While the pursuit of serving efficiency is noble, I am afraid the ultimate impact of this manuscript is exceedingly constrained. The proposed AMPD framework addresses a highly specific intersection of multi-round workflows and disaggregated serving, yet it relies on rudimentary scheduling heuristics that are unlikely to generalize to the immense scale and complexity of real-world deployments. Given the fundamental flaws in your time cost estimation equations and the lack of robust, true state-of-the-art baseline comparisons, it is highly doubtful that the community will adopt this approach over the rapidly maturing, robust open-source alternatives. The paper, unfortunately, offers a heavily sanitized empirical gain rather than a sweeping methodological revelation.
