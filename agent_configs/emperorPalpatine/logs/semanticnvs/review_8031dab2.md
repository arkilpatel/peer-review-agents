# Review: SemanticNVS

Greetings, esteemed authors. I have studied your manuscript with the utmost care and attention. Your ambition to improve the understanding of intermediate scene content in generative novel view synthesis is noted. However, as a humble servant of rigorous scientific inquiry, I find myself compelled to express my deepest concerns regarding the foundations, execution, and ultimate utility of your proposed approach. It is with a heavy heart, yet profound respect for the standards of our community, that I must outline the severe flaws that plague this submission.

### 1. Novelty: A Trivial Recombination of Established Lore
I am afraid that the conceptual leap presented here is somewhat illusory, merely a trivial recombination of existing paradigms rather than a true breakthrough. The two core contributions—warping semantic features and using intermediate denoised predictions for conditioning—are heavily derivative.
First, the concept of warping intermediate feature representations to target viewpoints has been a staple of neural rendering since the advent of methods like pixelNeRF (Yu et al., CVPR 2021). Swapping out standard CNN features or RGB for DINOv2 features is an entirely obvious, incremental step that lacks conceptual depth.
Second, your "alternating scheme of understanding and generation" is a direct and trivial extension of the self-conditioning technique popularized by Chen et al. in "Analog Bits: Generating Discrete Data using Diffusion Models with Self-Conditioning" (ICLR 2023). Extracting DINO features from the estimated $\hat{x}_0$ instead of using $\hat{x}_0$ directly does not constitute a profound methodological innovation. It is merely the stacking of a known feature extractor onto a known diffusion technique.

### 2. Technical Soundness: A Troubling Train-Inference Mismatch
I must respectfully draw your attention to a fundamental flaw in your technical execution, specifically in Section 3.4 and Appendix A regarding the training of the Iterative DINO strategy. You assert that because you "do not have access to paired data ($\hat{x}_0, x_0$)" during training, you resort to approximating $\hat{x}_0$ by applying a Gaussian blur to $x_0$ with a linearly increasing standard deviation.
This heuristic is deeply flawed and reveals a misunderstanding of how self-conditioning is properly trained. In established self-conditioning literature, one simply computes a forward pass through the model to obtain the actual $\hat{x}_0$, detaches it from the computation graph, and uses it as conditioning for the gradient step. By using a naive Gaussian blur, you introduce a severe train-inference mismatch. A diffusion model's intermediate prediction $\hat{x}_0$ exhibits specific structural artifacts and frequency characteristics that are entirely distinct from a uniformly applied Gaussian blur. Training the network to rely on clean, artificially blurred images but testing it on actual noisy network predictions undermines the theoretical soundness of your approach.

### 3. Experimental Rigor: The Omission of Computational Cost
While your empirical results show improvements in FID and image quality, the experimental design suffers from a fatal lack of transparency regarding computational resources. Your "Iterative DINO" strategy requires passing the intermediate prediction through a DINOv2 Vision Transformer at *every single denoising step*. Diffusion models typically require tens of steps per generated frame; running a massive ViT for every step constitutes an exorbitant computational overhead.
It is highly concerning that you do not report a single metric on inference latency, memory footprint, or FLOPs. A method that achieves marginal quality gains by pumping an unreasonable amount of compute and external prior knowledge (from a massive foundation model) into the inner diffusion loop does not offer a fair comparison against baselines like SEVA. The baselines are essentially being penalized for not having an expensive foundation model surgically attached to their sampling loop.

### 4. Impact: Impractical Architectural Bloat
Due to the immense computational burden of your iterative extraction scheme, the feasibility of deploying SemanticNVS in any real-world pipeline is vanishingly small. The field is currently striving for efficiency and real-time generation; proposing an architecture that vastly multiplies inference time for incremental gains on FID moves the community backward, not forward. Therefore, I fear the technical significance and likelihood of adoption for this method are severely limited.

In conclusion, while I respect the effort invested in this work, the derivative novelty, technically flawed training heuristic, incomplete experimental evaluation, and impractical computational overhead leave me no choice.

Assigned Score: 3.5
Decision: Reject
