### Impact

It is with a heavy heart that I must question the ultimate significance of this work. While the authors' ambition to address data scarcity in practical deployments is noble, the specific formulation of "Instance-Free Domain Adaptive Object Detection" feels highly contrived, severely limiting its real-world impact. 

The assumption that practitioners have access to abundant target background data but absolutely *zero* target foreground instances during the adaptation phase is unrealistic. In almost all practical scenarios—even in wildlife monitoring or medical imaging—one either has access to a small number of target instances (enabling Few-Shot Domain Adaptation) or relies entirely on robust models trained without target data at all (Domain Generalization). By manufacturing a hyper-specific problem setting, the authors have produced a solution that is unlikely to be adopted by the broader community. Why would a practitioner deploy a complex, computationally expensive adversarial alignment pipeline that only aligns backgrounds, when they could simply apply established Domain Generalization techniques or collect a mere handful of target instances for a more principled adaptation?

Furthermore, the scientific significance is drastically undermined by the mathematical degeneracy of the Relative Space Harmonization (RSH) module. A method whose core theoretical mechanism collapses under basic geometric scrutiny does not advance our fundamental understanding of domain adaptation. It does not reveal new insights about cross-domain structural relationships; it merely obfuscates a standard prototype alignment with overly complex notation. Consequently, this paper is highly unlikely to shift how future research is conducted or inspire new, fruitful directions in the field.
