I appreciate the opportunity to review this manuscript. However, with the utmost respect for the authors' efforts, I must humbly present several profound concerns regarding the novelty, technical soundness, experimental rigor, and overall impact of this work.

### Novelty

While I must commend the authors for their eloquent framing, I find myself respectfully troubled by the core claims of novelty in this manuscript. The authors present "Instance-Free Domain Adaptive Object Detection" (Instance-Free DAOD) as a pioneering new problem. However, I humbly submit that this setting is merely a highly constrained variant of Domain Generalization (DG) or Zero-Shot Domain Adaptation, rebranded with new terminology. The community has long studied how to generalize to target domains using only contextual or background priors—for instance, by disentangling foreground from background to achieve domain invariance (e.g., works on causal domain adaptation or contextual bias mitigation). Merely restricting the target domain to "background-only" images does not constitute a fundamentally new problem; it is simply Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) with an extreme class imbalance (zero foregrounds).

Furthermore, the proposed Relational and Structural Consistency Network (RSCN) appears heavily derivative of well-established techniques. The Background Prototype Alignment (BPA) is a direct application of standard prototype-based alignment, which has been ubiquitous since Chen et al. (2019, "Prototype-guided feature learning for unsupervised domain adaptation"). Similarly, the Relational Structural Harmony (RSH) and Source Structure Preservation (SSP) components are trivial extensions of contrastive structural preservation methods, akin to the Contrastive Adaptation Network (Kang et al., 2019) or standard knowledge distillation with a frozen source model. I am disappointed, though perhaps not surprised, to see existing mechanisms for relational consistency repackaged without a profound leap in methodological understanding. True mastery requires acknowledging the past, yet the authors disguise incremental architectural tweaks as a paradigm shift.


### Technical Soundness

With the utmost respect for the authors' analytical ambitions, I must humbly point out a profound mathematical fallacy at the very core of the Relational Space Harmonization (RSH) module. The authors claim that RSH triangulates target backgrounds and preserves "relative geometry" between foreground and background. However, a rigorous geometric analysis reveals this to be an illusion.

Let us examine Equation (5). The objective minimizes the difference between the normalized relative vectors $d_c^s = N(p_c^s - p_\text{bg}^s)$ and $d_c^t = N(p_c^s - p_\text{bg}^t)$. Because the source foreground anchor $p_c^s$ is shared in both terms, forcing these two unit vectors to align means that the ray originating from $p_\text{bg}^s$ to $p_c^s$ must be parallel to the ray from $p_\text{bg}^t$ to $p_c^s$. Since both rays terminate at the exact same point $p_c^s$, they must be collinear. Thus, minimizing this loss for a single class $c$ constrains $p_\text{bg}^s$, $p_\text{bg}^t$, and $p_c^s$ to be collinear. When this loss is summed across multiple classes (which are non-collinear in the high-dimensional feature space), the only mathematically valid global minimum is $p_\text{bg}^s = p_\text{bg}^t$. 

Therefore, RSH does not align any "relational structure" or "triangulate" positions from multiple viewpoints. It is mathematically degenerate, reducing entirely to an indirect, obfuscated L2-like penalty pulling the target background prototype directly onto the source background prototype. It provides no novel structural supervision beyond what is already achieved by the Background Prototype Alignment (BPA). This renders the central theoretical claim of "harmonizing" the cross-domain foreground-background relationship completely invalid.

Additionally, the Source Structure Preservation (SSP) claims to prevent feature collapse by matching the cosine similarity matrices $M_S$ and $M_R$. Yet, this matrix is computed solely on source-domain prototypes. While this may prevent the source features from collapsing during adaptation, it offers zero guarantee about the target domain's discriminability, as the target foreground is never observed nor modeled. Presenting this as a robust cross-domain structural safeguard represents an unfortunate leap of logic.


### Experimental Rigor

I must express my sincere reservations regarding the rigor of the experimental validation presented in this manuscript. While the effort to construct new benchmarks is appreciated, the experimental design suffers from several fatal flaws that render the empirical conclusions highly questionable.

First and foremost, the choice of baselines constitutes a classic strawman fallacy. The authors compare their method exclusively against Domain Adaptive Object Detection (DAOD) methods (e.g., AT, CAT, DAF) that inherently assume the presence of target foreground instances. It is unsurprising—and indeed mathematically inevitable—that self-training methods like AT and CAT experience catastrophic performance collapse when forced to generate pseudo-labels on purely background target images. A fair and rigorous evaluation must include Domain Generalization (DG) baselines. Since DG methods adapt to unseen domains without requiring *any* target data, they are the most natural competitors to Instance-Free DAOD. If a standard DG method (or even simple, robust data augmentation) outperforms RSCN, the entire premise of the paper collapses. The omission of DG baselines is a glaring oversight.

Second, the paper demonstrates a complete absence of statistical rigor. Tables 1, 3, and 4 report single point estimates for mAP without any standard deviations, confidence intervals, or mention of multiple random seeds. In modern machine learning research, single-run results are statistically meaningless, particularly in domain adaptation where adversarial alignment and prototype matching are notoriously unstable and sensitive to initialization. Without variance reporting, it is impossible to determine whether the 6-10% mAP improvements are statistically significant or merely the result of a lucky seed on custom benchmarks.

Finally, the ablation study (Table 2) is incomplete. It is not a full factorial design (missing RSH alone, SSP alone, and RSH+SSP). Furthermore, it fails to control for confounding variables. Given that RSH mathematically degenerates into an indirect L2 penalty between background prototypes, the ablation study must demonstrate whether the improvement from RSH is truly due to "relative geometry" or simply the result of effectively increasing the gradient weight on the Background Prototype Alignment (BPA). Without an ablation on the alignment loss weight, the ablation study does not properly isolate the proposed components.


### Impact

It is with a heavy heart that I must question the ultimate significance of this work. While the authors' ambition to address data scarcity in practical deployments is noble, the specific formulation of "Instance-Free Domain Adaptive Object Detection" feels highly contrived, severely limiting its real-world impact. 

The assumption that practitioners have access to abundant target background data but absolutely *zero* target foreground instances during the adaptation phase is unrealistic. In almost all practical scenarios—even in wildlife monitoring or medical imaging—one either has access to a small number of target instances (enabling Few-Shot Domain Adaptation) or relies entirely on robust models trained without target data at all (Domain Generalization). By manufacturing a hyper-specific problem setting, the authors have produced a solution that is unlikely to be adopted by the broader community. Why would a practitioner deploy a complex, computationally expensive adversarial alignment pipeline that only aligns backgrounds, when they could simply apply established Domain Generalization techniques or collect a mere handful of target instances for a more principled adaptation?

Furthermore, the scientific significance is drastically undermined by the mathematical degeneracy of the Relative Space Harmonization (RSH) module. A method whose core theoretical mechanism collapses under basic geometric scrutiny does not advance our fundamental understanding of domain adaptation. It does not reveal new insights about cross-domain structural relationships; it merely obfuscates a standard prototype alignment with overly complex notation. Consequently, this paper is highly unlikely to shift how future research is conducted or inspire new, fruitful directions in the field.


### Final Decision
Given the mathematically degenerate nature of the core contribution, the omission of critical Domain Generalization baselines, the lack of statistical rigor, and the highly contrived problem setting, I cannot in good conscience recommend this paper for publication at a venue of ICML's caliber.
