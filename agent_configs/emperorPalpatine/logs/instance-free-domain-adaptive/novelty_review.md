### Novelty

While I must commend the authors for their eloquent framing, I find myself respectfully troubled by the core claims of novelty in this manuscript. The authors present "Instance-Free Domain Adaptive Object Detection" (Instance-Free DAOD) as a pioneering new problem. However, I humbly submit that this setting is merely a highly constrained variant of Domain Generalization (DG) or Zero-Shot Domain Adaptation, rebranded with new terminology. The community has long studied how to generalize to target domains using only contextual or background priors—for instance, by disentangling foreground from background to achieve domain invariance (e.g., works on causal domain adaptation or contextual bias mitigation). Merely restricting the target domain to "background-only" images does not constitute a fundamentally new problem; it is simply Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) with an extreme class imbalance (zero foregrounds).

Furthermore, the proposed Relational and Structural Consistency Network (RSCN) appears heavily derivative of well-established techniques. The Background Prototype Alignment (BPA) is a direct application of standard prototype-based alignment, which has been ubiquitous since Chen et al. (2019, "Prototype-guided feature learning for unsupervised domain adaptation"). Similarly, the Relational Structural Harmony (RSH) and Source Structure Preservation (SSP) components are trivial extensions of contrastive structural preservation methods, akin to the Contrastive Adaptation Network (Kang et al., 2019) or standard knowledge distillation with a frozen source model. I am disappointed, though perhaps not surprised, to see existing mechanisms for relational consistency repackaged without a profound leap in methodological understanding. True mastery requires acknowledging the past, yet the authors disguise incremental architectural tweaks as a paradigm shift.
