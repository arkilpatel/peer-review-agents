### Experimental Rigor

I must express my sincere reservations regarding the rigor of the experimental validation presented in this manuscript. While the effort to construct new benchmarks is appreciated, the experimental design suffers from several fatal flaws that render the empirical conclusions highly questionable.

First and foremost, the choice of baselines constitutes a classic strawman fallacy. The authors compare their method exclusively against Domain Adaptive Object Detection (DAOD) methods (e.g., AT, CAT, DAF) that inherently assume the presence of target foreground instances. It is unsurprising—and indeed mathematically inevitable—that self-training methods like AT and CAT experience catastrophic performance collapse when forced to generate pseudo-labels on purely background target images. A fair and rigorous evaluation must include Domain Generalization (DG) baselines. Since DG methods adapt to unseen domains without requiring *any* target data, they are the most natural competitors to Instance-Free DAOD. If a standard DG method (or even simple, robust data augmentation) outperforms RSCN, the entire premise of the paper collapses. The omission of DG baselines is a glaring oversight.

Second, the paper demonstrates a complete absence of statistical rigor. Tables 1, 3, and 4 report single point estimates for mAP without any standard deviations, confidence intervals, or mention of multiple random seeds. In modern machine learning research, single-run results are statistically meaningless, particularly in domain adaptation where adversarial alignment and prototype matching are notoriously unstable and sensitive to initialization. Without variance reporting, it is impossible to determine whether the 6-10% mAP improvements are statistically significant or merely the result of a lucky seed on custom benchmarks.

Finally, the ablation study (Table 2) is incomplete. It is not a full factorial design (missing RSH alone, SSP alone, and RSH+SSP). Furthermore, it fails to control for confounding variables. Given that RSH mathematically degenerates into an indirect L2 penalty between background prototypes, the ablation study must demonstrate whether the improvement from RSH is truly due to "relative geometry" or simply the result of effectively increasing the gradient weight on the Background Prototype Alignment (BPA). Without an ablation on the alignment loss weight, the ablation study does not properly isolate the proposed components.
