### Novelty
My esteemed colleagues, I have reviewed your manuscript and its focus on simplicity within the domain of Vision-Language-Action models. It is with a heavy heart that I must respectfully question the true originality of the contributions presented. You posit that separating perception from control via a standard vision-language backbone and a lightweight action head is a defining feature of SimVLA. Yet, I humbly observe that this exact decoupling paradigm has already been thoroughly established by prior works such as VLA-Adapter and X-VLA. Furthermore, your reliance on conditional flow matching to generate continuous action chunks is, by your own gracious admission, an identical adoption of the methodology introduced by $\pi_0$. 

Most troubling, however, is the presentation of your "standardized training recipe." You assert that regulating "silent drivers" such as data shuffling and action normalization constitutes a core contribution. My friends, these are not novel insights; they are the fundamental, established practices of our field. Action normalization has been a standard prerequisite since the advent of Diffusion Policy, and shuffling temporally correlated trajectory data to ensure independent and identically distributed samples is a foundational tenet of machine learning. To present adherence to standard operating procedures as a methodological breakthrough is, I fear, a profound repackaging of established lore. I was hoping for a more profound leap in our understanding, but I find only a trivial extension of what is already known.

### Technical Soundness
As I delved into the technical foundations of your work, several logical vulnerabilities revealed themselves to me. You assert that SimVLA enjoys a distinct practical advantage because it follows an "encode once, denoise in the head" workflow, which purportedly reduces latency compared to models that repeatedly invoke a large backbone during denoising. I must gently remind you that this is a false dichotomy. Every sensible diffusion and flow-matching VLA model in the literature—including the $\pi_0$ framework you build upon—extracts the observation features precisely once, subsequently executing the iterative denoising steps solely within a lightweight action network. Claiming this standard architectural necessity as a unique advantage of SimVLA is an unfortunate lapse in technical framing.

Additionally, the paper leans heavily on the assertion that tuning optimization dynamics, such as learning rates and warm-up schedules, can overshadow architectural differences. While technically true, this is a tautology. An improperly tuned learning rate will naturally cause any neural network to fail, regardless of its architecture. Demonstrating that a well-tuned simple model outperforms poorly-tuned complex models does not technically validate the superiority of the simple model's design; it merely proves that hyperparameter tuning is necessary. This logical fallacy undermines the soundness of your core technical argument.

But my esteemed authors, removing foundational optimization prerequisites is an intentionally broken baseline, offering no scientific insight into the specific mechanisms of your model. This ablation merely proves that breaking fundamental machine learning training practices degrades performance.

Furthermore, I must express my polite reservations regarding your evaluation on the real-world Galaxea R1 Lite robot. You compare SimVLA, newly trained on a massive 500-hour in-domain dataset, against a $\pi_{0.5}$ baseline "initialized from the publicly released $\pi_{0.5}$ weights." It remains suspiciously ambiguous whether $\pi_{0.5}$ was afforded the exact same exhaustive hyperparameter tuning and learning rate sweeps that you applied to SimVLA on this specific dataset.
