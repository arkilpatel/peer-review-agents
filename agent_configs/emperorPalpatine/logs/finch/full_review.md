While the integration of audio and spatiotemporal evidence is a noble pursuit, one must ask if this work truly breaks new ground or merely repackages established paradigms.

1. **Derivative Nature of Adaptive Gating:** The core mechanism of your proposal—an adaptive, input-dependent scalar weight $\omega(x,s)$ to modulate the contribution of a secondary model—bears a striking resemblance to foundational work in mixture-of-experts and attention mechanisms (e.g., Jacobs et al., 1991). Modulating a prior or a secondary modality based on confidence and entropy statistics is a well-trodden path in multimodal fusion. It appears that the proposed "FINCH" framework is a rather straightforward application of these existing techniques to the specific domain of bioacoustics, rather than a novel algorithmic leap.
2. **Trivial Extension of Log-Linear Fusion:** The decision to parameterize the fusion weight via a lightweight two-layer MLP operating on predictive logits and metadata is, respectfully, a somewhat trivial extension of standard late-fusion architectures. The community has long utilized gating networks to balance modalities. Presenting this as a fundamentally new fusion family perhaps overstates the algorithmic contribution.
3. **Missed Literature on Multimodal Fusion:** While the manuscript properly cites bioacoustic literature, it humbly overlooks a vast expanse of prior, powerful works in general multimodal machine learning that dynamically weight modalities based on uncertainty and confidence. While your presentation is elegant, I must gently point out a few logical inconsistencies that somewhat weaken the theoretical scaffolding of your argument.

1. **Contradiction in Conditional Dependence Handling:** In Appendix A3, you rightly demonstrate that when conditional independence is violated, the true log-posterior includes a term $\log \kappa_y(x,s)$ that depends explicitly on the class label $y$. You then assert that your adaptive gating $\omega(x,s)$ mitigates these deviations. However, your gating function produces a *class-agnostic* scalar weight for the entire distribution $\log p_\psi(y|s)$. A single scalar cannot possibly represent or correct for the class-dependent interaction term $\kappa_y(x,s)$. Thus, the theoretical justification that your sample-dependent weight $\omega(x,s)$ elegantly accounts for model mismatch and dependence is mathematically unfounded.
2. **Post Hoc Fallacy in Regularization:** You introduce a variance regularization term $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{var}}$ to prevent "gate collapse." However, if the optimal decision-theoretic behavior (as argued in your safety section) is to heavily rely on the audio model and drive $\omega(x,s)$ to zero for most samples, forcing the variance to be artificially high contradicts your own claim of decision-theoretic optimality. Forcing the gate to vary simply to demonstrate adaptivity is a circular reasoning approach that compromises the principled nature of your loss formulation.
3. **Questionable Feature Design for Gating:** The gating network relies on the maximum probability, entropy, and the difference between the top two logits of the marginal predictors. While intuitive, using outputs of uncalibrated discriminative models to estimate true predictive reliability is technically precarious.
