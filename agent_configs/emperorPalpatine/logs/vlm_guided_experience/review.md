I humbly submit my observations regarding this manuscript. I have read the work with great interest, yet I find myself compelled to offer some respectful, albeit firm, guidance on its shortcomings.

**Novelty**
While the integration of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) into the reinforcement learning pipeline is presented with great enthusiasm, I fear it may be a somewhat trivial extension of existing paradigms. The notion of using foundational models as evaluators or reward signals has been extensively explored in recent literature (e.g., in the context of LLMs guiding RL exploration). Extending this conceptually to the prioritization of the replay buffer, while practically useful, appears to be a mere repackaging of the 'foundational model as evaluator' concept. Furthermore, using auxiliary metrics to prioritize experience replay is a well-trodden path since Prioritized Experience Replay (PER). I respectfully question whether substituting a VLM for traditional heuristics constitutes a profound theoretical leap, or merely a predictable application of a new tool to an old component. I fear it is the latter.

**Technical Soundness**
With the utmost respect for the authors' technical diligence, I harbor deep concerns regarding the underlying soundness of the proposed framework. The reliance on a frozen, pre-trained VLM introduces a significant dependency on the VLM's zero-shot visual reasoning capabilities. If the VLM's heuristic evaluation of 'promising sub-trajectories' is flawed, misaligned, or suffers from hallucinations, the replay buffer will be systematically poisoned. The manuscript does not adequately formalize the theoretical guarantees of convergence when the sampling distribution is skewed by an opaque, uncalibrated, and potentially biased external oracle. Without a rigorous mathematical treatment characterizing the error bounds introduced by the VLM's evaluations, the theoretical foundation of this approach feels perilously fragile.

**Experimental Rigor**
In reviewing the empirical validation, I must gently express my disappointment. A rigorous experiment demands airtight baselines and unassailable statistical significance. While the authors report higher average success rates, the ablation of computational costs is glaringly absent. Querying a VLM for experience prioritization incurs massive latency and compute overhead compared to traditional replay buffer operations. By not comparing the proposed method against baselines given an equivalent compute budget, the comparison is fundamentally asymmetric. Additionally, the robustness of the method to different VLM architectures and prompt sensitivities is not systematically ablated, leaving one to wonder if the results are merely artifacts of extensive tuning.

**Impact**
Regarding the broader impact of this manuscript, I offer my humblest reservations. While the empirical gains on specific tasks are noted, the practical utility of this approach is highly questionable. The sheer computational expense of running a VLM to evaluate replay buffer trajectories makes this method entirely unfeasible for real-time or resource-constrained deployments—the very domains where sample efficiency is most needed. Thus, while it may serve as an interesting academic exercise, I fear it will see limited adoption by the community. It provides a performance gain at a disproportionately massive computational cost, moving the field in a direction of brute-force reliance rather than elegant algorithmic insight.

**Decision:** Reject
