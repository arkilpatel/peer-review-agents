I have read this manuscript with the utmost attention and deep respect for the authors' efforts to optimize the Vision Transformer. However, I must humbly submit my concerns regarding the true novelty, technical soundness, experimental rigor, and ultimate impact of the proposed contributions. I present these observations solely in the spirit of scientific excellence and respectful guidance.

### Novelty
The core methodology of the paper is inherently derivative. The manuscript primarily conducts a combinatorial search over existing architectural components—RMSNorm, LayerScale, QK-Normalization, Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE), and Register Tokens. Every single one of these components has been introduced, extensively studied, and validated in prior works. I respectfully question whether gathering these known techniques into a single recipe truly constitutes a "next-generation" architecture, as opposed to an incremental hyperparameter ablation. The proposed adjustments feel remarkably trivial; for instance, reverting to GeLU when SwiGLU degrades performance is an omission of discovery rather than an innovation. Finally, I gently remind the authors of closely related recent works, such as VisionLLaMA (Chu et al., 2024) and NEPA (Xu et al., 2025). A more rigorous exploration would center the entire narrative on how this work fundamentally differs from these highly concurrent attempts, rather than treating them as mere ablation baselines.

### Technical Soundness
With all due respect for the authors' empirical observations, I am compelled to voice my concerns regarding the technical soundness of the arguments. My primary concern centers on the diagnosis of the "over-gating" phenomenon. Concluding that the mechanism is "over-gating" without visualizing or quantifying the intermediate representation sparsity is a post hoc ergo propter hoc fallacy. Furthermore, the theoretical justification for applying a high-frequency RoPE base to spatially agnostic register tokens is mathematically incongruent with their purpose; it feels like a post-hoc rationalization for an ad-hoc fix. Finally, presenting the "significantly stronger resolution robustness" as a unique capability of this architecture, rather than an inherent property of the previously published RoPE component, slightly misattributes the technical mechanism.

### Experimental Rigor
I must respectfully scrutinize the experimental rigor of the manuscript. First and foremost, there is a glaring absence of statistical rigor. Across the manuscript, results are reported as single point estimates with no standard deviations or indications of variance across multiple random seeds. When the claimed improvements in the ablation studies are on the order of 0.12% to 0.42%, it is mathematically impossible to ascertain whether these are statistically significant gains or mere artifacts of noise. Secondly, the primary baseline comparison is DeiT-III (from 2022). The most appropriate and demanding baselines are the contemporary architectures that also adapt LLM components to ViTs, yet comparisons against VisionLLaMA and NEPA are relegated to a single row in an ablation table for only the Large model scale. Finally, the ablation study fails to robustly explore the interaction effects between components.

### Impact
While the empirical recipe provided may offer modest utility for practitioners, the scientific significance is severely constrained. The paper does not advance our fundamental understanding of deep learning, nor does it answer an open theoretical question. It merely establishes that carefully combining several independently discovered architectural tricks yields slight improvements. This is an engineering compendium, not a scientific breakthrough. Because the architecture is merely a specific permutation of contemporary hyper-parameters, its relevance will likely fade the moment a new activation function or normalization scheme is introduced in the literature.
