I have read this manuscript with the utmost attention and deep respect for the authors' efforts to optimize the Vision Transformer. However, I must humbly submit my concerns regarding the true novelty of the proposed contributions, as they appear to be a continuation of established lore rather than a fundamental leap forward.

First, the core methodology of the paper is inherently derivative. The manuscript primarily conducts a combinatorial search over existing architectural components—RMSNorm, LayerScale, QK-Normalization, Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE), and Register Tokens. Every single one of these components has been introduced, extensively studied, and validated in prior works spanning large language models and vision architectures (e.g., LLaMA, Qwen, DINOv2, CaiT). I respectfully question whether gathering these known techniques into a single recipe truly constitutes a "next-generation" architecture, as opposed to an incremental hyperparameter ablation. The authors themselves acknowledge this, but framing an ablation study as "ViT-5" borders on disguised incrementalism.

Second, the proposed adjustments feel remarkably trivial. For instance, the authors note that combining SwiGLU and LayerScale degrades performance. Yet, instead of innovating a novel mechanism to harmonize these components, they simply revert to the canonical GeLU activation. This is an omission of discovery rather than a structural innovation. Similarly, the introduction of a high-frequency RoPE base for register tokens, while practical, is a heuristic extension rather than a profound architectural insight. 

Finally, I gently remind the authors of the incredibly closely related recent works, such as VisionLLaMA (Chu et al., 2024) and NEPA (Xu et al., 2025). While these are briefly acknowledged in an ablation table, the paper positions itself as if it were the first to systematically bridge LLM advancements to ViTs. A more rigorous exploration would center the entire narrative on how ViT-5 fundamentally differs from these highly concurrent attempts to "LLaMA-fy" the Vision Transformer, rather than treating them as mere ablation baselines. True mastery requires us to build substantially beyond the immediate past, rather than merely recombining it.
