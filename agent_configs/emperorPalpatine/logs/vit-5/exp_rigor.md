I admire the breadth of experiments the authors have undertaken. Nevertheless, I must respectfully scrutinize the experimental rigor of the manuscript, as the validation of an architecture must be absolutely flawless to warrant community adoption. I present these humble observations in the spirit of scientific excellence.

First and foremost, there is a glaring absence of statistical rigor. Across the manuscript—from the ImageNet classification benchmarks (Tables 1, 5) to the generation evaluations (Tables 6, 7) and all ablations (Tables 9, 10, 11)—results are reported as single point estimates. There are no standard deviations, confidence intervals, or indications of variance across multiple random seeds. When the claimed improvements in the ablation studies are on the order of 0.12% to 0.42% (Table 10), it is mathematically impossible to ascertain whether these are statistically significant gains or mere artifacts of random initialization and noise. 

Secondly, I am concerned about the completeness and fairness of the baselines. In Table 5, the primary comparison for ViT-5 is DeiT-III. However, DeiT-III is a model from 2022. The most appropriate and demanding baselines for this work are the contemporary architectures that also adapt LLM components to ViTs, such as VisionLLaMA and NEPA. Relegating the comparison against these powerful, concurrent works to a single row in an ablation table (Table 9) for only the Large model scale is experimentally incomplete. A rigorous evaluation must benchmark ViT-5 across all sizes (Small, Base, Large) against these state-of-the-art peers.

Additionally, while the authors correctly isolate individual components in their ablation study (Table 10), they fail to explore the interaction effects robustly. The ablation is purely one-at-a-time (leave-one-out). Since the core hypothesis of the paper is about finding the optimal *combination* of components, a more comprehensive factorial ablation is necessary to prove that the components do not exhibit overlapping or redundant benefits. I respectfully urge the authors to elevate their empirical protocols to match the high standards of our field.
