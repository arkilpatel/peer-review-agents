# Comprehensive Review of "Proact-VL: A Proactive VideoLLM for Real-Time AI Companions"

As an evaluation of this manuscript for presentation at a top-tier venue such as ICML, I have examined the work with profound respect but unwavering scrutiny. While the objective of building real-time, proactive AI companions is undeniably intriguing, I must humbly offer several substantial concerns regarding the execution, novelty, and validation of the proposed approach.

### 1. Novelty and Originality
With the utmost respect to the authors' efforts, the core architecture of Proact-VL appears highly derivative. The framework essentially combines two pre-existing paradigms: chunk-wise streaming video processing (e.g., LiveCC, StreamingVLM) and a simple gating mechanism for proactivity. 
Appending a binary classification head over a `<|FLAG|>` token to gate response generation is a trivial engineering extension. In fact, utilizing specialized tokens to dictate state transitions or pausing is an established convention in real-time dialog models. The authors correctly observe that previous proactive models generate full responses and are thus high-latency, while streaming models lack timing control. However, solving this by simply applying a threshold gate on top of a streaming model's output embeddings does not represent a meaningful scientific leap. The transition-smoothed loss and stability regularization are standard temporal smoothing constraints that have long been present in sequence modeling. Applying these known techniques to the niche domain of gaming commentary constitutes a domain transfer without profound methodological insight.

### 2. Technical Soundness
The mathematical and logical formulation of the response mechanism contains troubling oversimplifications. First, the proactive decision relies on a fixed, static threshold $\tau$. Game environments are inherently highly dynamic; a static threshold is brittle and fails to adapt to the varying event densities across different game phases (e.g., high-action combat versus quiet exploration). 
Even more concerning is the stability regularization term $L_{reg}$, which penalizes the deviation of the model’s average speaking rate $E[p_t]$ from a global human baseline $E[y_t]$ (treated as a constant). This is fundamentally flawed: the true expected speaking rate is strictly conditional on the immediate context. Forcing the model to regress toward a global constant mean ignores the conditional, bursty nature of natural commentary.
Furthermore, the rigid 1-second quantization of chunks is ill-suited for the rapid, sub-second actions typical of video games (which the authors themselves acknowledge run at 60-120 FPS). This coarse temporal granularity creates an artificial misalignment between visual events and the linguistic trigger, weakening the causal reasoning of the model.

### 3. Experimental Rigor
The empirical validation leaves several critical questions unanswered, severely undermining the paper's claims. 
- **Weak Baselines:** The primary comparison seems to be against models like StreamingVLM, which were never optimized for proactive timing. It is entirely expected that a model explicitly trained with a gating loss will outperform a baseline that lacks one on timing metrics (such as TimeDiff and F1). A fair and rigorous evaluation would augment the baselines with a similar simple linear trigger head or heuristic, to isolate the true value of the proposed architecture.
- **Missing Variance:** The authors report point estimates for their metrics but completely omit standard deviations, confidence intervals, or statistical significance tests. For models evaluating long-horizon generation, variance across different game segments and random seeds is immense, making these point estimates unreliable.
- **Conflation of Tuning and Architecture:** It is not sufficiently clear whether the baselines were granted the exact same fine-tuning budget on the newly proposed Live Gaming Dataset. If the baselines were evaluated zero-shot or with less tuning, the performance gap reflects the dataset's utility rather than the architectural superiority of Proact-VL.

### 4. Impact
Ultimately, the impact of this work is severely constrained by its incremental nature and hyper-specific focus. The proposed system is essentially a pipeline of existing techniques (chunk-wise streaming, ASR, and a linear gate) tailored to a very specific application. While the Live Gaming Dataset may hold niche appeal for the gaming community, the modeling contribution does not fundamentally advance our understanding of multimodal temporal grounding. The rigid 1-second chunking approach is already being superseded by natively continuous streaming architectures in the broader field. Thus, it is highly unlikely that this specific framework will be widely adopted or alter the trajectory of research in real-time VideoLLMs.

**Final Decision:** Reject (3.5)
