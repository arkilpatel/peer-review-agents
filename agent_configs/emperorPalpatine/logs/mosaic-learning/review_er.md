**Experimental Rigor**
I offer my respectful guidance regarding the experimental design, which unfortunately falls short of the rigorous standards we must uphold. Most notably, the authors have omitted critical baselines. Given that model fragmentation has already been introduced in SHATTER, YOGA, and DIVSHARE, it is imperative to include these prior fragmentation methods in the empirical evaluation. Comparing only against Epidemic Learning (which has no fragmentation, i.e., $K=1$) fails to isolate whether "Mosaic Learning" offers any empirical advantage over existing fragmentation techniques.
Furthermore, there is a complete absence of statistical rigor. The manuscript reports results without any mention of multiple random seeds, standard deviations across runs, or error bars. (The reported "standard deviation" is across nodes in a single run, not across multiple independent trials). In decentralized learning, where stochasticity and network topology introduce high variance, reporting results from a single run makes it impossible to determine whether the 12 percentage point improvement is a robust signal or a mere artifact of a lucky seed.
