### Novelty and Originality

While I acknowledge the authors' ambition to scale interactive world models to 1000-frame horizons, I must respectfully express my humble concerns regarding the true novelty of the proposed methodologies. A careful examination reveals that the core contributions are largely derivative, repackaging established paradigms from adjacent fields without fundamentally advancing the theoretical frontier of sequence modeling or generative representation.

**1. Derivative Nature of the Hierarchical Memory Compressor:**
The "Hierarchical Pose-free Memory Compressor (HPMC)" is presented as a novel mechanism for bounding computational cost. However, the paradigm of recursively distilling historical latents into a fixed-budget representation is a direct continuation of established long-context memory architectures. For example, the *Compressive Transformer* (Rae et al., 2019) formalized the exact concept of compressing fine-grained past memories into coarser, bounded representations to maintain constant complexity over extended horizons. Applying this established temporal compression scheme to visual latents within a DiT backbone—while engineering a two-stage (local and global) forward pass—feels like a domain transfer without profound new insight. It is a straightforward adaptation of hierarchical sequence modeling to video diffusion, lacking the algorithmic leap one expects at this venue.

**2. Triviality of the Action Labeling Module:**
I must gently point out that the "Uncertainty-Aware Action Labeling" module, characterized by its "tri-state logic," is an exceedingly trivial extension of standard pseudo-labeling heuristics. Categorizing continuous motion into bins (e.g., No-operation, Discrete Action, and Uncertain) using two static thresholds ($\tau_1$ and $\tau_2$) is rudimentary data sanitization, not a methodological breakthrough. Discarding or isolating uncertain signals is ubiquitous in semi-supervised learning and robust optimization. Furthermore, discretizing continuous camera pose changes into "WASD" and directional bins has been heavily utilized in prior embodied AI literature (e.g., *Video PreTraining*, Baker et al., 2022) to map video to discrete action spaces. Rebranding these basic thresholding and bucketing heuristics as a core architectural innovation is a missed opportunity for a deeper, more principled approach to handling noisy real-world trajectories.

**3. Over-claiming in the Finetuning Strategy:**
Finally, the "Revisit-Dense Finetuning Strategy" is presented as a guided insight to activate loop-closure capabilities. Yet, the premise—that a model must observe loop-closures (revisits) in its training distribution to accurately generate them during inference—is a foundational truism of machine learning. Curating a 30-minute dataset containing these specific visual phenomena is a sensible data engineering step, but classifying it as a novel "strategy" or core contribution over-claims its scientific weight. I respectfully submit that true mastery of long-horizon world modeling requires more than just showing the model the exact behaviors we wish it to mimic during a fine-tuning stage.
### Technical Soundness

While I find the aspiration to simulate 1000-frame horizons highly commendable, I must respectfully raise several grave concerns regarding the technical soundness and logical consistency of the claims presented in the manuscript. I fear there are fundamental contradictions between the stated theoretical guarantees and the mathematical realities of the proposed architecture.

**1. The "Constant Computational Cost" and "Fixed Memory Budget" Fallacy:**
The authors claim that the Hierarchical Pose-free Memory Compressor (HPMC) achieves a "fixed memory budget" and "constant computational cost" over long horizons. However, examining Mode 1 (Equation 1) reveals that the temporal encoder $f_\phi$ downsamples the sequence by a constant factor of $k=4$, yielding output of length $L/4$. In Mode 2 (Equation 2), the raw latents are chunked into $N$ windows, passed through $f_\phi$, concatenated, and passed through $f_\phi$ again. If $f_\phi$ fundamentally applies a $4\times$ reduction, the length of the final representation $z_{com}$ is proportional to $N \times W / 16$. Unless the number of chunks $N$ is strictly capped (which would mean $S$ grows indefinitely), the sequence length still scales linearly with the input horizon $L$. If the authors achieve a "strictly bounded memory footprint" by dynamically increasing the stride $S$ as $L$ grows, they are essentially performing uniform temporal downsampling at the chunk level. This directly contradicts their criticism in Section 2.2, where they accurately dismiss uniform downsampling for incurring "severe information loss." The mathematics do not support a magical "constant cost" without severe degradation for infinite horizons.

**2. The Contradictory "Pose-Free" Claim:**
The manuscript repeatedly emphasizes that the framework operates in a "purely data-driven, pose-free manner," explicitly claiming to eliminate the need for geometric priors (Section 3.1). However, in the very next section (Section 3.2), the authors detail their "Uncertainty-Aware Action Labeling" module, which relies heavily on an "off-the-shelf pose estimator" to calculate 6-DoF pose changes ($\Delta P$). These pose-derived estimates are then discretized into translation and rotation magnitudes and fed back into the model via the Action Encoder. Generating the conditioning signal using explicit geometric pose estimation completely undermines the claim that the model is "pose-free." The memory compressor may not explicitly take poses as input, but the generative backbone relies fundamentally on pose-derived action tokens to structure its space. This is a severe conceptual inconsistency.

**3. Unjustified Causal Claims Regarding HPMC's Optimization:**
The authors claim that because HPMC is "jointly optimized end-to-end," the model "autonomously identify and preserve the most relevant historical cues for loop-closure." There is no formal proof or rigorous empirical isolation provided to prove that the joint optimization objective naturally forces the retention of *loop-closure* specific features rather than generic texture or color averages. To make this causal claim, one would need to inspect the attention maps or gradients of the compressor specifically during loop-closure events, rather than attributing the macroscopic success of the system entirely to this unaudited mechanism. The gap between what is hypothesized (autonomous loop-closure routing) and what is actually shown is vast.
### Experimental Rigor

I must respectfully express my deep reservations regarding the experimental methodology and the empirical substantiation of the manuscript's core claims. The gap between what is promised in the abstract and what is actually measured in the experiments is alarmingly wide.

**1. Omission of Objective Consistency Metrics:**
The central thesis of this paper is the model's ability to maintain "coherent visual memory" and "spatial consistency" over 1000-frame horizons, specifically enabling "loop-closure." However, in Section 4.2.3, the authors explicitly state: "we deliberately exclude subject consistency and background consistency from our assessment." By removing the very VBench metrics designed to measure consistency, the authors have completely stripped the paper of any objective, automated evaluation of their primary contribution. Relying entirely on a subjective User Study to evaluate "Memory Consistency" is unacceptable for a methodological claim of this magnitude. The authors could have easily measured the LPIPS, PSNR, or CLIP similarity between the first frame and the final frame of a known loop-closure trajectory to objectively quantify memory drift. The failure to include standard, objective loop-closure metrics severely undermines the credibility of the 1000-frame consistency claim.

**2. Insufficient Action Trajectory Diversity:**
In Section 4.2.1, the authors describe constructing a benchmark using 100 initial frames but only "10 representative long action trajectories." This means the model's action-responsiveness and memory consistency are evaluated on a remarkably narrow set of just 10 distinct motion paths, applied across different starting images. Ten trajectories are vastly insufficient to robustly evaluate an interactive world model meant to handle open-domain, complex real-world exploration. This narrow evaluation suite risks severe overfitting and fails to prove that the model's "Uncertainty-aware Action Labeling" generalizes to arbitrary user inputs.

**3. Contradictions Between Claims and Results:**
The abstract boldly claims that "Infinite-World achieves superior performance in visual quality, action controllability, and spatial consistency." Yet, an examination of Table 1 reveals that these claims are empirically contradicted by the paper's own data. First, the baseline *Yume 1.5* outperforms Infinite-World in the VBench Average Score (0.8141 vs. 0.8119) and Aesthetic Quality (0.5840 vs. 0.5440). Second, the baseline *HY-World-1.5* outperforms Infinite-World in the User Study for Action Responsiveness (ranking 1.50 vs. 1.54, where lower is better). Claiming "superior performance" across the board when the model is demonstrably beaten by existing baselines on key automated metrics and in action controllability is highly misleading and lacks scientific candor.

**4. Ambiguity in User Study Aggregation:**
The methodology for the User Study describes participants evaluating "randomized pairs of results" (A/B testing). However, Table 1 reports average rankings (e.g., 1.92, 2.43) for Memory, Fidelity, and Action across five models. It is mathematically and methodologically opaque how pairwise preference choices were translated into these specific absolute continuous rankings. Without a clear explanation of the aggregation method (e.g., Bradley-Terry modeling), these numbers appear arbitrary and their statistical significance cannot be verified.
### Significance and Impact

When evaluating the potential impact of this work on the broader scientific community, I must respectfully express my doubts that this manuscript will meaningfully shift the trajectory of interactive world model research.

**1. Limited Technical Utility:**
The stated goal—to achieve stable, action-responsive 1000-frame simulation—is undeniably an important open problem. However, the proposed solution relies heavily on a highly specific pipeline: taking off-the-shelf pose estimates, discretizing them via arbitrary thresholding into a "tri-state" logic, and then fine-tuning on a curated 30-minute dataset of stable iPhone footage. This pipeline is brittle and lacks the elegance or generalizability required for widespread adoption. Future research is far more likely to pursue end-to-end continuous action conditioning and scalable unsupervised learning, rather than reverting to manually thresholded, discrete pseudo-labels. Thus, the technical utility of this exact architecture is severely limited.

**2. Marginal and Contradictory Empirical Gains:**
For a method to have high scientific impact, its empirical superiority must be unambiguous. As noted earlier, the model fails to outperform the baseline *Yume 1.5* on the overall VBench average score and aesthetic quality, and it lags behind *HY-World-1.5* in action responsiveness. The improvements over baselines, where they exist, are marginal or confined solely to subjective human rankings with opaque aggregation methodologies. A method that claims a breakthrough in long-horizon generation but cannot unequivocally dominate the objective state-of-the-art across standard automated metrics is unlikely to be adopted as a new foundational architecture.

**3. Absence of Fundamental Insights:**
Scientifically, the paper does not reveal a critical failure mode of prior work, nor does it present a novel theoretical framework. It merely demonstrates that concatenating a known hierarchical compression scheme with a small, curated dataset containing loop-closures allows a 1.3B parameter diffusion model to roughly simulate longer horizons. It answers no fundamental open questions about the nature of generative memory or continuous control. Consequently, I fear this work will be viewed as a modest engineering exercise rather than a seminal contribution that changes how the field understands world modeling.
