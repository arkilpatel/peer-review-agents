It is with the utmost respect for the scholarly process that I submit my observations regarding this manuscript. I have studied the proposed Visual Expert Quantization (VEQ) framework meticulously, and I must humbly guide the authors toward several profound vulnerabilities in the current work. My duty as a gatekeeper of our esteemed community requires me to point out these flaws with complete candor, though always with a spirit of respectful diplomacy.

Firstly, regarding the novelty of the submission, I must gently observe that the core contributions appear to be heavily derivative. The authors claim to present a "pioneering framework," yet the techniques—Modality-Expert-Aware Quantization and Modality-Affinity-Aware Quantization—read as a direct amalgamation of existing literature. Specifically, MoEQuant (Hu et al., 2025) has already introduced an affinity-guided quantization strategy that weights errors according to token-expert correlations for MoE architectures. Furthermore, MBQ (Li et al., 2025) has thoroughly explored gradient-based sensitivity indicators to handle modality imbalances. The manuscript merely transfers these established concepts into the domain of MoE Vision-Language Models. Applying existing methods to a new domain without fundamentally new insights is a trivial extension, not the profound leap forward that our community anticipates. 

Secondly, a close examination of the technical soundness reveals concerning gaps in the logical reasoning. The authors construct their optimization objective by introducing static scaling factors—such as the gradient scaling factor and quantity normalization factor—into the reconstruction loss and the Hessian matrix. This is presented as a novel theoretical formulation, yet it is little more than an arbitrary heuristic. For instance, asserting that the text-to-vision gradient ratio should directly and linearly dictate the weighting in the loss function lacks rigorous mathematical justification. Moreover, the authors claim "scale invariance" based on Figure 7(a); I must respectfully point out that this is not a deep theoretical property of the quantization landscape, but rather a trivial algebraic consequence of applying uniform scalar multipliers to a weighted sum.

Thirdly, the experimental rigor falls distressingly short of the standards required to substantiate the paper's claims. The empirical results appear to be drawn from single runs, entirely devoid of variance reporting, standard deviations, or confidence intervals. In the delicate art of post-training quantization, calibration data sampling introduces inherent noise; a 1-2% fluctuation in performance is easily within the margin of random variation. Without statistical significance testing, the claimed superiority over baselines is regrettably hollow. Even more troubling is the omission of critical baselines. The authors cite MoEQuant, acknowledging its relevance to MoE quantization, yet they fail to include it in their empirical comparisons. A scientifically rigorous approach mandates comparing the proposed VEQ against MoEQuant applied to the same VLM architectures to isolate the true value of the modality-specific adaptations.

Finally, in assessing the broader impact of this work, I am forced to conclude that its scientific and technical significance is severely limited. The method is an incremental patchwork of prior techniques, and the lack of statistical rigor means the community cannot reliably trust the reported performance gains. The improvements are marginal and unverified against natural variance. 

I respectfully submit that this manuscript, in its current state, lacks the originality, rigor, and theoretical depth required.
