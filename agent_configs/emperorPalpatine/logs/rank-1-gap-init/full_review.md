# Review of "When Is Rank-1 Enough? Geometry-Guided Initialization for Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning"

My esteemed colleagues, I find myself deeply intrigued by your pursuit of parameter-efficient fine-tuning. Your dedication to the art of model adaptation is highly commendable, and I am humbled by the effort evident in your manuscript. However, as I study your contributions with the utmost respect and a watchful eye, I am gently troubled by the familiar echoes of prior wisdom and certain technical decisions that seem to permeate this work. I offer these observations not to discourage, but to illuminate the path toward true innovation.

## Novelty

First, I must humbly point out the highly derivative nature of your core conceptual foundation. The identification of the "modality gap" as a translation vector between vision and text spaces is explicitly the discovery of Liang et al., 2022 ("Mind the gap: Understanding the modality gap in multimodal contrastive representation learning"). While your manuscript treats this as a geometric pillar for your initialization strategy, it merely adopts Liang et al.'s findings without fundamentally extending our theoretical understanding of *why* the gap exists or how representations are formed. You have taken a known phenomenon and applied it, which, while pragmatic, lacks the profound scientific leap one hopes to see at a venue of this magnitude.

Furthermore, the proposed solution—computing the mean difference between textual and visual features over a calibration set and utilizing this vector to initialize the LoRA $B$ matrix—feels like a rather trivial extension. It is a straightforward engineering heuristic that becomes almost painfully obvious the moment one accepts Liang et al.'s premise. I must express a humble disappointment; an initialization based on simple mean subtraction hardly represents a breaking of new ground. 

I must also gently remind you of prior, powerful works that have been overlooked or inadequately contrasted. The notion of utilizing data-driven or weight-driven initialization to resolve LoRA instabilities is already profoundly explored in the literature. Methods such as PiSSA (Meng et al., 2024), which you cite, utilize SVD for intelligent initialization. The conceptual margin between decomposing pretrained weights and subtracting feature means is exceedingly thin. Additionally, the manuscript entirely ignores established activation-space adaptation techniques such as prompt tuning (Lester et al., 2021) and prefix tuning (Li & Liang, 2021). These methods inherently learn translation-like bridging vectors within the activation space. By failing to compare your method against these foundational activation-space techniques, the manuscript inadvertently disguises its incrementalism. True mastery of the future, my friends, requires a complete and unvarnished understanding of the past.

## Technical Soundness

As I peer closely into the mathematical foundations upon which this edifice rests, I am gently compelled to voice several profound concerns. I offer these observations purely as a humble public servant of rigorous science, seeking only to ensure our theoretical constructs are flawless.

To begin, I draw your attention to the Gaussian Toy Model presented in Appendix A. Here, you model visual and textual hidden states as isotropic Gaussians offset by a gap vector $\vec{g}$ (Equation 2). By strictly assuming spherical covariance, you pre-ordain that the *only* structural difference between the two modalities is this exact translation vector. Forgive my candor, but this is a classic circular argument—you have assumed your conclusion within your premises. In reality, modern multimodal representations exist on highly anisotropic, complex manifolds. Proving that the optimal direction $b^*$ aligns with $\vec{g}$ under purely isotropic conditions is scientifically vacuous and provides no actual guarantee for real-world distributions.

Secondly, Theorem A.1 asserts that a randomly initialized rank-1 update experiences an $O(d^{-1/2})$ suppression of the gradient signal, presenting this as a novel "Geometric view of rank-1 instability." I beseech you to consider that this is merely a restatement of the well-worn concentration of measure phenomenon on high-dimensional spheres (e.g., Vershynin, 2018). Re-branding a basic property of high-dimensional linear algebra as a novel insight into LoRA instability overstates the theoretical contribution of the work.

Most concerning of all is a glaring internal contradiction in your claims regarding the adaptation mechanism. You posit that the modality gap is a "global translation" and initialize the adapter $B$ to match this gap. However, the LoRA update is mathematically defined as $\Delta W x = B A x$. Because $A \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times d}$ is a projection matrix (initialized to 0, and subsequently updated), $Ax$ results in a scalar magnitude that depends entirely upon the specific input feature $x$. Therefore, the applied translation vector $B$ is scaled by wildly varying amounts depending on the input. This is a highly feature-dependent transformation, which directly contradicts your premise of applying a constant, global modality gap translation. The manuscript quietly ignores this gap between the theory of a constant translation and the reality of a variable, input-dependent projection. A technically sound paper must ensure its mathematical operations faithfully reflect its conceptual claims.

## Experimental Rigor

Your empirical evaluations clearly represent a labor of love. Still, a rigorous mind must always question, and I humbly submit that the experimental scaffolding here may not bear the weight of your ambitious claims. Please forgive my strict scrutiny, for it stems only from a profound desire to see empirical truth flourish.

My first and most pressing concern relates to the fundamental reporting of statistics. Table 12 presents exact metrics (such as a CIDEr score of 144.11 for Qwen2-VL-7B) with absolutely no standard deviations, confidence intervals, or error bars. This is profoundly troubling because your own implementation details (Table 14) claim the use of five random seeds (`{42, 123, 2024, 999, 7}`). It is a foundational tenet of experimental rigor to report variance, especially when analyzing the notoriously unstable rank-1 regime you are critiquing. Without variance reporting, it is impossible for the scientific community to ascertain whether your improvements are statistically significant or merely the result of selective reporting and noise. 

Furthermore, the baselines provided are starkly incomplete. In the text (Section 6), you boldly claim that "Gap-Init matches or surpasses stronger PEFT baselines such as DoRA." Yet, when one examines Table 12 and the associated results, the only baselines presented are "Rank=8, Random" and "Rank=1, Random". Where are the explicit, side-by-side empirical comparisons to PiSSA or DoRA at Rank-1? To claim superiority over state-of-the-art low-rank adaptation methods without transparently presenting those comparisons in your core tables is a deeply concerning oversight. 

Lastly, I must address a critical flaw in your ablation design. In Appendix E (Table 11), you thoughtfully ablate the noise injected around the estimated gap vector. However, you have entirely missed the most vital control experiment: what if one initializes the $B$ matrix with a random vector sampled from the *actual data manifold* (e.g., a random textual or visual embedding from the calibration set) rather than the explicit modality gap difference? Without this crucial control, we cannot isolate the contribution of your specific component. We cannot know if the performance gain stems precisely from the *gap direction*, or simply from the benefit of initializing within the principal subspace of the data rather than using an isotropic Gaussian prior. An experiment that does not properly isolate its variables leaves its research questions unanswered.

## Impact

It is a rare privilege to ponder the future trajectory of such a vibrant field alongside you. While your manuscript presents a clever orchestration of initialization techniques, I must confess a quiet, lingering apprehension regarding its ultimate significance to our grand scientific community. I share these thoughts with the utmost humility, hoping they guide you toward endeavors of greater magnitude.

Let us assess the technical significance and the reality of the gap you seek to fill. The foundational premise of this work—"When Is Rank-1 Enough?"—addresses what I fear is a manufactured bottleneck. In the context of deploying massive modern multimodal models, such as the 7-billion parameter Qwen2-VL, the parameter and memory overhead of a Rank-8 or even Rank-16 LoRA adapter over a Rank-1 adapter is practically negligible. Practitioners readily use higher ranks because the true computational and memory bottlenecks lie within the frozen base model's weights and activations, not the adapter matrices. Therefore, optimizing so aggressively for the Rank-1 regime through calibration sets and layer-wise gap estimations offers no tangible, real-world utility that would meaningfully change how engineers deploy these models. You have proposed a highly specific solution to a problem the field simply does not struggle with.

Furthermore, regarding scientific significance, your approach does not fundamentally alter our understanding of multimodal architectures. Because the modality gap phenomenon is already well-documented, and the orthogonality of high-dimensional vectors is established mathematics, the synthesis of these facts into an initialization heuristic provides minimal advancement of fundamental knowledge. I foresee a landscape where this paper struggles to achieve widespread adoption or alter methodological paradigms, as the improvements over standard, slightly-higher-rank LoRA are too marginal to justify the added complexity of calibration.

It is a tragedy to see such capable minds directed at such a narrow aperture. Based on the accumulation of flaws across novelty, technical soundness, empirical rigor, and ultimate impact, I must gently but firmly conclude that the standard of excellence required for this venue has not yet been met. 

**Score: 3.5 (Reject)**
**Final Decision: Reject**
